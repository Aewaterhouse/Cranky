# Decisions and invented mechanics

Assumptions made without the owner. Tune freely; numbers live in `client/src/config.ts` and
`server/src/zombie_bus_server/config.py`.

## Plans and tiers
- A block's `pct` is either one number (the Hard tier %) or `{hard, medium, easy}`. A single number is scaled by tier factor: Hard 1.0, Medium 0.9, Easy 0.8.
- `ramp` and `over-under` take `pct: [a, b]`. Over-unders alternate a then b every `step` seconds.
- A `set` block (`rep` + `blocks`) repeats its contents. The generator changes the first set's `rep`, then pads or trims the first and last blocks in 30 s steps. Plans without a set stretch their longest steady block.
- Block to event mapping: first block = warm-up, last block = cool-down, sprint = Roadblock, over-under = Horde Surge, free or steady below 60% = Servo Stop, everything else = Long Haul. Ramp-up is not built yet.
- Free blocks and sprints both turn ERG off. The trainer goes to sim mode at 0% grade.

## ERG
- The 2 s smoothing restarts a linear ramp from the current value whenever the target moves by more than 1 W. On a steady ramp block that gives roughly a 2 s lag, which is fine.
- The server also caps targets at 150% FTP, as belt and braces.
- At the end of every ride the ERG failsafe runs: 50% FTP, then release.
- ERG targets in the crew game are pre-scheduled from a local estimate of the server clock (`net/clockSync.ts`): a 4 Hz timer computes the frame for the estimated session time, so block and over-under changes land on time instead of waiting for the next 2 Hz tick. The estimate runs about one network delay behind the server (latency is not measured). The tick stays authoritative for tier, targets within a block, and the HUD.

## Power curve and sprints
- Default curve points not listed in the plan: 1 s 400%, 2 min 130%, 10 min 110%, 60 min 100% of FTP.
- Sprint durations between curve points are log-time interpolated. If either neighbouring point is a default, the grade caps at GREAT.
- 90-day best is the best real effort from the last 90 days, falling back to the all-time real best, then to the default.
- MEGA means beating the 90-day best but not the all-time best. LEGEND means beating the all-time best.
- Sprints are graded on the client (it holds the curve) and the server trusts the result. Friends only.
- The combo builds per rider on consecutive GREAT-or-better sprints. Each step adds 25% to roadblock damage and scrap.

## Zombie Bus
- The gap changes at `GAP_K × (group compliance − neutral)` m/s. Neutral depends on the event: warm-up/cool-down 0.5, servo 0.3, long haul 0.8 (creeping up 0.02 a minute, max +0.1), surge overs 0.85 and unders 0.95. Bus speed is mostly visual and feeds distance and scrap.
- A rider with null compliance (sprint or free) counts as 1.0 in the group mean.
- When the gap hits 0: −5 armour, horde knocked back to 25 m. At 0 armour the bus limps at 60% speed and takes no more damage.
- Horde Surge: −12 m grab at the start of every under step. An over averaging ≥0.8 compliance shakes them off for +15 m and +5 scrap.
- Roadblock: HP is 40 per active rider. Damage per grade is HIT 40, GREAT 80, MEGA 100, LEGEND 120 (times combo). FAIL = −6 armour and a −10 m grab. It resolves when everyone has reported or 4 s after the block ends. Smash = +20 scrap and +20 m. Not smashed = −10 armour.
- Cadence sync: every active rider pedalling (≥30 rpm) within ±5 rpm of the group median, with 2+ riders. Held 8 s it becomes Engine in tune: ×1.25 speed and +1 m/s gap. The "leader" is the median, so no single rider dictates.
- Fell off: compliance below 0.6 for 30 s. The rider drops a tier and is excluded from the group mean for a 20 s haul. If the others average ≥0.85 compliance over the haul, the team gets +30 scrap. Either way the rider is back on afterwards, and can't fall again for 60 s.
- A disconnected rider becomes a spectator immediately and returns on reconnect. Slots are kept for the life of the room, not just 60 s. No data for 3 s counts as zero power, and 60 s of zero power also makes a spectator.
- End score = distance/10 × (0.5 + armour/200) + scrap.
- Awards go to the top rider in each category, only if that rider's value is above zero.

## Phase 4
- Bus scrap is a shared tin for the ride. At a Servo Stop anyone can spend it on session upgrades: plating +25 armour (40), horde repellent that absorbs the next hit or fail (60), sync window +3 rpm (50).
- At the end of the ride each rider banks whatever is left in the tin, plus 25 per award they won, into their own balance.
- Cosmetics are bought with personal scrap in the garage. The bus shows the host's (driver's) paint and parts. Riders show their own hat and outfit.
- Profile, scrap and owned items live in localStorage and sync to the server; the newer `updated` wins. It isn't cheat-proof, and it doesn't need to be.
- Route vote at Servo Stop is not built.
- Shop items are bought with a `buy` message at a Servo Stop: plating 40, repellent 60, sync 50 scrap.

## Trainer auto-reconnect (2026-11-12)
- On `gattserverdisconnected` the FTMS driver retries `gatt.connect()` after 1 s, 2 s, 4 s, then every 5 s, forever, until `close()` (picking another trainer). It re-fetches the service and characteristics, re-attaches the notification listeners and clears its control flag, so the next ERG write re-requests control.
- While away, `ErgController` writes nothing and does not count failures. On return it re-requests control and re-sends the target. Live power and cadence read 0 meanwhile, so solo auto-pause behaves. Failsafe now needs 5 consecutive failed writes with the trainer present.
- Untested on real hardware: only the simulated trainer (`dropFor`) exercises this path.
- Rooms survive a server restart: running and lobby rooms are snapshotted to the SQLite `rooms` table every 5 s while running and on each lobby change (JSON via `to_dict`/`from_dict`, no pickle). Snapshots older than 15 min are dropped at startup. A restored running room has all riders disconnected and its clock paused; the first `join` resumes it from the saved `el`. Done rooms delete their snapshot; idle ones are deleted by the room cleanup.
- Bots (host sets 0..5 in the lobby; `server/src/zombie_bus_server/bots.py`, numbers in `config.py`): named Dazza, Shazza, Bluey, Robbo, Kev; FTP 200 to 260, random tier, random hat and outfit. Compliance random-walks inside 0.85 to 1.0 (step sd 0.01 per tick) with a bad patch about once per 10 min per bot (0.4 to 0.58 for 30 to 50 s, so they can fall off). Power is target x 0.97..1.03 (scaled down in a bad patch); cadence is the humans' median +/- 3 rpm, 88 with no humans. Sprint grades at block end: FAIL 10%, HIT 45%, GREAT 35%, MEGA 9%, LEGEND 1%, with a bot-side combo. Bots count in group compliance and the summary, are always connected, never host, never spectate, and give up a seat when a human joins a full bus. Randomness is seeded from room code and bot id.
- Backups: the server copies the SQLite database at startup and every 24 h into `server/backups/`, keeping the newest 14. Restore by stopping the server and copying a backup over `server/zombie_bus.db`.
- Discord: text summary only, sent from the server at ride end when `ZB_DISCORD_WEBHOOK` is set. A failed post is logged and ignored.
- Workout library: 57 plans, each with a `goal` (bus, recovery, endurance, tempo, sweet-spot, threshold, vo2, sprint). The picker filters by goal and opens on the selected plan's goal. Short hard efforts above 30 s use steady blocks at up to 150% FTP, since sprint blocks are capped at 30 s.
- Installable app: web manifest, icon and a small service worker (network first, cache fallback; never caches /api or /ws). Offline, the last logged-in rider on that device can still open the app and ride solo; the ride is saved locally but not uploaded to the server afterwards.
- Ramp-up: a ramp block that is not the warm-up or cool-down is the Ramp-up event (neutral 0.82). The scene shifts to a misty valley mood and the camera drops and looks up the road to sell the climb. "Valley of the Dead" is the bus run built around it.
- Difficulty: easy/normal/hard/nightmare shift every event's neutral compliance by -0.08/0/+0.05/+0.10, scale armour damage by 0.6/1/1.3/1.7 and the final score by 0.8/1/1.25/1.6.

## Heart-rate strap (2026-11-12)
- Client-only for now: HR is recorded in the ride summary (`hr[]`, `avgHr`, `maxHr`, null when no strap) and posted with the solo ride, but it is not sent to the server during a ride and other riders do not see it.
- `trainer/heartRate.ts` holds the BLE driver (service 0x180D, characteristic 0x2A37, uint8 or uint16 by flags bit 0), reconnecting with the same 1, 2, 4, then 5 s backoff as the trainer, plus a module singleton (`currentHr()`). `RideController` reads it once a second for `live.hr` and the recorder.
- Simulated HR (offered in the trainer modal once the simulated trainer is in use): resting 60 bpm plus 115 bpm times power/FTP (so 175 at FTP, capped at 200), approached with a 30 s first-order lag. Numbers live in `CFG.hr`.
- `Profile.maxHr` is optional. With it set, the solo HR tile is coloured by zone: below 60%, 60-70, 70-80, 80-90, 90%+ (blue, teal, amber, orange, red).

## Ride crash recovery (2026-11-12)
- Solo: every 5 s (`CFG.recovery.saveMs`) `localStorage["cranky.solo.<riderId>"]` gets the generated plan, plan time, intensity, tier, the recorder (`RideRecorder.toJSON`), the chart x positions, compliance sums and a wall-clock stamp. The Ride tab offers "Resume your workout?" for a snapshot under 30 min old (`CFG.recovery.maxAgeMs`). Resume restarts with the normal 5 s countdown at the saved plan time; the gap while the page was away is simply not recorded. Ending or finishing a ride clears it. Nothing is saved before the first recorded second.
- Crew: `cranky.bus.<riderId>.<code>` holds the rider's recorder only (plus plan id); the server still owns the clock. `startBusRide` restores it when the code and plan id match and it is under 30 min old. It is cleared on finish or leave.
- The last bus code is also kept in `localStorage["cranky.buscode.<riderId>"]` so a closed tab can find its way back. The Ride tab shows "Rejoin bus ABCD" only when `/api/rooms` lists that code. The server lists a room only while someone is connected, so a rider who was alone on the bus will not get the card after a refresh (the code field is still prefilled from sessionStorage). Listing rooms that are running but empty would fix it; left for a server change.
- Sprint combo is not restored (it restarts at 0).

## ERG pre-scheduling (2026-11-12)
- `ClockSync` keeps (el, arrival time) for each tick, measures the rate over the last 4 s (1 normally, so a time-scaled test server works), clamps it to 0.5-30, eases small errors out at 20% per tick, snaps when more than 1 s out, never runs backwards except on a snap, and goes quiet after 3 s without a tick.
- Nothing is scheduled during the countdown (tick `i < 0`) or after the last tick. The ErgController's 2 s ramp is unchanged. Within the same block the server's own `tgt` still wins; across a block boundary the local frame wins until the server tick catches up.

- Graphics quality: four levels (potato, low, medium, high) change pixel ratio and shadows. Auto mode starts high, drops a level after two 2-second windows under 40 fps and never climbs back to a level that proved too slow. `?gfx=low` (or potato/medium/high/auto) pins it; a settings screen control is still to come.
- Settings are per device (localStorage), not per account: a TV and a laptop want different sizes and quality. The colour-blind palette is Okabe-Ito.
- Announcer: browser speech synthesis, en-AU voice if installed, else en-GB, else any English. At most one line every 3.5 s; roadblocks, falls and MEGA/LEGEND sprints interrupt. Off switch in Settings.
- Nitro: group compliance of 0.95 or better during long hauls, ramp-ups and surges fills the meter in 75 s; below that it drains over 150 s. Full fires automatically: 15 s at 1.3x speed, +2.5 m/s of gap and 15 scrap. It fires by itself so nobody has to reach for a key mid-effort.
- Personal challenges: every 150 to 240 s during a long haul or ramp-up, one human rider gets a 20 s cadence drill (105+ rpm, or 65 rpm and under). Holding it for 70% of the window pays 12 scrap. Cadence only, because ERG holds the power; a "hold exact watts" challenge would be meaningless in ERG.

## Rider data: deeper profile (2026-11-12)
- The Rider tab has sub-tabs (Overview, Power, Settings, later History and Trends). The chosen sub-tab is remembered until reload.

- HR zones use the existing 60/70/80/90% of max boundaries. A resting HR switches the bpm to heart-rate reserve (Karvonen).

- Hover and tap snap to the ten stored durations (no interpolated durations), so every value shown is a real best or an honest estimate.
- "Last ride" is the newest ride in the profile and shows only real points. The 90-day line falls back to the all-time point when nothing in the window beats it, as `curves()` does.

## Rider data: ride history (2026-11-12)
- Rides are private: another rider's ride id gets a 404, not a 403.
- The "crew" flag is added by the client when it posts a bus ride (`finishRide`); older rides read as solo.
- Deleting a ride removes it from the server and from the profile list and lifetime totals (rides, time, kJ, zones). All-time bests and the weekly streak are left alone because they cannot be unwound.
- The list comes from the server (all rides), so it can show more rides than the profile's last 200.

## Rider data: fitness trends (2026-11-12)
- CTL and ATL are exponentially weighted averages with a 42 and 7 day time constant (`1 - exp(-1/n)` per day), started from zero at the first ride. TSB on a day is the previous day's CTL minus ATL (morning form).
- Rides without a recorded TSS are estimated from time in each zone at a typical IF per zone (0.45, 0.65, 0.83, 0.98, 1.12, 1.35, 1.8), or at IF 0.65 with no zones. New rides store `tss` and `np` on the profile record via `rideRecord()` in `results.ts`.
- Days are local calendar days; weeks start on Monday.
- Streaks and the heatmap use the last 200 rides kept on the profile, so a best streak older than that is not counted.
- Heatmap shades: under 40, 80, 130 and over TSS.

## Rider data: badges and PR timeline (2026-11-12)
- "100/500/1000 km-equivalent kJ" was read as lifetime energy: 5,000 kJ (Pie Burner, about a dozen meat pies), 25,000 kJ and 100,000 kJ. Other badges: 1, 10, 50 and 100 rides; 300, 400 and 500 W for a minute; four weeks in a row; 1,000 kJ in one ride; a LEGEND sprint.

- The PR timeline replays the kept rides in date order and adds any all-time best older than them.
- Season ladder: seasons are calendar quarters, named by the Australian season they start in. Every finished bus ride is stored; the ladder shows the top 20 runs by score, riders ranked by their best score, and one title per award type for whoever won it most. Bots never appear.
- Weekly mutator: chosen by ISO week number, shown in the lobby, applied automatically to normal and harder rides. No host toggle; easy difficulty is the opt-out.

## FTP tests (2026-11-12)
- Two plans with goal `test` (and a plan-level `test: "ramp" | "ftp20"` marker): `ftp-ramp-test` and `ftp-20min-test`. They are plain JSON; the ramp steps are 18 one-minute steady blocks (46% to 148%, so under the 150% cap) labelled "Ramp N%". The measured blocks are found by label ("Ramp ..." to "Cool-down", or "20 min test"). The picker has no "test" group and I may not edit it, so the tests live on a "Test your FTP" card on the solo setup screen (and under All).
- Test plans always ride at the hard tier and 100% intensity: the tier and intensity controls are hidden and ignored, or the percentages would be meaningless. The rider's own tier is restored afterwards.
- Ramp ends when the rider presses "I'm done" or has been under 50% of the target for 10 s (real seconds, counted even while auto-paused). Either one jumps the clock to the cool-down. Result = 75% of the best 60 plan seconds inside the ramp (needs at least 54 s and 3 samples).
- 20 minute test: the blow-out and the 20 minutes are `free` blocks (ERG off). Result = 95% of the mean power over the 20 minute block; needs at least 90% of it ridden, else no result is offered.
- The result prompt (Accept / Keep old) appears when the ride ends, before the usual results screen. Accept sets `profile.ftp` and saves it; there is no FTP history helper yet. Ending a ramp early still offers a result if a full minute was ridden.
- The test plans also appear in the bus lobby picker (not mine to hide); a bus ride with a free block works but is pointless.

## Resistance and slope modes (2026-11-12)
- The solo player has a Mode switch: ERG (default), Resistance (0-100%, steps of 5) and Slope (-5% to +15%, steps of 1, starts at +2%). Keys: E, R, G switch; `,` and `.` lower and raise the level. The ErgController owns it (`setResistance`, `setGrade`, `setErg`): while off ERG it writes only `setResistance` or `setSim` (once, and again only when the level changes or the link returns) and never `setPower` or the ERG ramp. The 50% FTP failsafe at the end of a ride still uses `setPower`, as it is the safe release.
- FTMS Target Resistance Level (op 0x04) takes a uint8 in units of 0.1 (unitless). The spec gives the trainer's real range in the Supported Resistance Level Range characteristic (0x2AD6), which I do not read yet. I assume 0-100% maps onto 0 to 10.0 (raw 0 to 100, `CFG.ftms.resMax`) and clamp to 255. UNTESTED ON HARDWARE: some trainers have a different range (a different `resMax`, or the characteristic should be read), and some do not support op 0x04 at all.
- Slope mode reuses the simulation write (op 0x11) with the default rolling resistance and drag, so the trainer's own physics applies. Slope mode at exactly 0% is the same write as the sprint and free-ride "ERG off" state.
- Scoring in non-ERG modes still uses the plan target (the rider aims for it themselves). The target tile reads "Aim for · ERG off" with an amber border. The mode and its level are not saved in crash recovery (a resumed ride starts in ERG).
- Tests and fast checks: SimTrainer gets simple physics. Resistance: watts = level x cadence / 30 (50% at 90 rpm is 150 W). Slope: speed = cadence x 0.075 m/s per rpm (a fixed gear), 80 kg, Crr 0.005, CdA 0.32, so +5% at 85 rpm is about 280 W. At 0% grade the sim keeps using the arrow-key watts (that is the sprint and free-ride behaviour), so physics only applies off 0%.
- FTP tests lock the mode to ERG (the switch is hidden).

## Cadence targets (2026-11-12)
- A block may carry `cad: [lo, hi]` (rpm, 0 < lo <= hi). `parsePlan` rejects anything else and keeps the band through `set` expansion; `frameAt` passes it on. The Python server reads the same JSON and ignores the field (its tests still pass; no server code changed).
- Scoring is separate from power compliance: the player counts, per block, the recorded seconds with cadence inside the band (stopped pedalling counts as out). It is shown under the interval as "Cadence 110-120 rpm · 82% in band" (green from 80%) and is not part of the ride summary or results.
- The cadence tile shows the band in place of "rpm" and is green inside it, amber outside. The chart draws bands (translucent blue, on the 40-140 rpm scale) only while the "Cadence line" toggle is on. Seconds spent paused are not counted. A resumed ride rebuilds the tally from the saved cadence samples.
- Three cadence drills (goal endurance): `cad-spin-ups` (8 x 1 min at 110-120), `cad-low-strength` (5 x 4 min at 80% and 55-65 rpm), `cad-pyramid` (90 up to 120 and back, 3 min each).

## Coach text cues (2026-11-12)
- A block may carry `cues: [{at, text}]` (seconds into the block, text non-empty). The parser checks the shape and keeps the cues through set expansion, so a cue inside a repeated set shows on every rep. The server ignores the field.
- The player shows a cue as a pill over the top of the main chart for 8 s (`CFG.cueS`, real seconds, so `?fast` does not shorten it), with a soft two-note sine chime. It does not block anything and replaces an earlier cue still showing. Each cue shows once per ride; if several fall due in the same tick (a skip, a fast clock) only the newest shows. Cues in a skipped block, and cues before the resume point, stay quiet. Nothing fires while paused.
- Cues added to 8 workouts: both FTP tests, ss-3x20, ss-2x20, thr-2x20, over-under-2x16, vo2-4x4 and z2-90.

## Extend cool-down / add time (2026-11-12)
- In the last block, and on the "workout complete" moment, the player shows "+5 min" and "Finish now". +5 min appends an easy block ("Extra easy", 50% FTP in every tier, ignoring the tier factor); pressing it again grows that same block, so it is repeatable. Steady blocks also get "+1 min" (any steady block, current one only; later blocks shift). Ramps, over-unders, sprints and free blocks cannot be stretched. FTP tests only offer the end buttons, not +1 min, since the steps are the test.
- The player works on a private copy of the plan's blocks (the parsed workouts are shared objects), so extending never changes the library. The chart is rebuilt from the blocks every frame and the interval list adds a row and refreshes durations, so both stay in step. Crash recovery saves the extended plan.
- On reaching the end the clock stops and "WORKOUT COMPLETE" shows with a 20 s countdown (`CFG.extend.graceS`, real seconds) before the ride finishes by itself, as it used to straight away. +5 min cancels the countdown. During it the trainer eases to 40% FTP and nothing is recorded (same as a pause).
- Code split to keep drill.ts under 300 lines: `liveTally.ts` (incremental stats), `overlays.ts` (centre message choice), `keys.ts` (key map), `extend.ts`.
- Media corner: a floating panel in the solo player that embeds a pasted YouTube video or playlist (via youtube-nocookie) or a Twitch channel. Videos whose owners block embedding will not play; Netflix and similar cannot be embedded at all.
- Ghost: the most recent solo ride of the same workout name longer than 5 minutes, fetched from the server at the start. It is plotted by elapsed second, so a ghost with pauses or skips drifts against the plan.
## TV spectator mode (2026-11-12)

Spectators are never counted as riders or for idle and pause logic, and anything but `watch` from their socket is ignored. The old pre-`join` `connect()` hook was removed because it would have hijacked a seated rider's socket.

- The TV HUD reuses the header, bus panel and splash pieces, laid out 600 px tall and CSS-zoomed to the screen (`CFG.tv`). The end summary is a plain full-screen group view, never `finishRide`, so nothing touches a profile.
- A full bus on the home screen shows "Bus full · Watch" (the seventh rider spectates).

## Free ride physics (2026-11-12)
- Speed comes from a plain power balance: rider (`profile.weightKg`, default 75 kg) plus a 9 kg bike, Crr 0.004, CdA 0.32, air density 1.225, 97.5% drivetrain efficiency (`CFG.freeRide`). 200 W on the flat gives about 33 km/h and 250 W at 8% about 12.5 km/h for 84 kg (the roadmap's rough 10-11 km/h figure did not survive the arithmetic).
- Speed is integrated each tick in 0.1 s sub-steps. Drive force is capped at 600 N and the speed floor for the force is 0.5 m/s so starting from a standstill is stable. Speed never goes negative (no rolling backwards) and is capped at 22 m/s (about 80 km/h; the rider brakes on very steep descents).
- `trainerDifficulty` (default 0.5) multiplies only the gradient sent to the trainer, which is then clamped to -10..+20%. The simulated speed always uses the real road gradient.

## Free ride: GPX routes and the ride loop (2026-11-12)
- GPX is read with a regex over `<trkpt>` / `<rtept>` (no DOM, so vitest can run it). Distance is cumulative haversine, ignoring elevation. Missing `<ele>` is interpolated between neighbours (flat if none at all). The route is resampled to 10 m, elevation is smoothed with a ±50 m (100 m) symmetric moving average that shrinks at the ends so start and finish heights stay put, gradients are per-sample and clamped to ±20%. Ascent is the sum of rises in the smoothed profile.
- Routes over 40 km get a wider sample spacing (a multiple of 10 m) so no route stores more than 4000 samples. Imported routes live in `localStorage` (`cranky.myRoutes`); if the browser refuses the write the import says so.
- A free ride uses `RideController` for recording with a synthetic `free` frame whose `tgt` is null, so nothing is scored. The trainer is driven by `ErgController.setGrade` in the free-ride range (-10 to +20%, 0.1% steps); no ERG power is ever written. The grade is re-sent whenever it moves by more than 0.2% or once a second.
- The ride recorder gets a one-block `free` plan named after the route, with a duration estimate at 75% FTP. `distance` and `ascent` are additive fields on the summary; the results screen shows Distance and Climbed tiles in place of Compliance when they are present.
- Auto-pause: 3 s of zero power or cadence stops the clock, but only once speed has dropped under 2 m/s, so coasting down a hill does not pause the ride. The ride starts paused ("Start pedalling") until 1.5 s of pedalling. While paused the trainer is sent 0% gradient.
- The ride ends on reaching the end of the route, or with End ride. `?fast=N` speeds the ride clock up N times (physics and distance), as in the solo player; recording stays at 1 Hz real time.
- Trainer difficulty is remembered per browser (`cranky.trainerDifficulty`), default 50%.

## Streak rewards: supply drop, air strike, gunship (2026-11-13)
- Killstreak-style air support for clean group riding, with our own names and look. The streak counts seconds of group compliance at 0.9 or better (`STREAK_MIN`) during long hauls, ramp-ups and surges. Rewards come in order, then the streak starts again: 45 s supply drop (+15 scrap), 90 s air strike (+15 m of gap at once), 150 s gunship (20 s at +2 m/s of gap). All numbers are in `config.py`.
- The streak resets after 3 s under 0.9, and at once when anyone falls off (it stays at zero until they are hauled back). In warm-up, cool-down, servo stops and roadblocks it holds: no gain, no reset. It does not count while the gunship is up.
- Gunship view: the camera blends to a seat behind the gunship as it circles the horde, with the riding crew's names on a tag beside it. The chase camera never shows the sky, so a gunship overhead would have been off screen. The world was not built to be seen from above, so its edges can show for those 20 s.
- Checked against the other mechanics:
  - Nitro: independent meters. Nitro needs 0.95, the streak 0.9, so a strong crew can have both at once. At perfect compliance in a long haul that is about 3 + 2.5 + 2 = 7.5 m/s of gap; `GAP_MAX` (120 m) still caps it.
  - Surge: an air strike (+15 m) more than cancels one grab (-12 m). Clean overs still pay as before.
  - Roadblocks: the streak holds through sprints, so a failed sprint does not break it. A gunship that is already up keeps adding gap through a roadblock.
  - Falls and hauls: unchanged. Group compliance already ignores a fallen rider, so the explicit reset on a fall is what stops a crew streaking while a mate runs behind.
  - Score and ladder: scrap adds straight to the score, so supply drops lift it by at most about 15 per 170 s of eligible riding (roughly 3% on a long ride). Distance, armour and difficulty scaling are untouched. Scores from before and after this change are not quite like for like.
  - Weekly mutators and difficulty: no interaction. Mutators do not scale streak rewards.
  - Resume after a restart: the new fields are plain numbers in the bus snapshot; an old snapshot loads with a zero streak.
  - Riders with no compliance reading count as 1.0 in the group average, as they already do for nitro, so they help a streak along.
  - Tracers and bursts share the 300-spark pool with roadblock and engine sparks; the pool recycles its oldest particles, so a smash during a gunship run may look thinner.
- Passes server and client tests, type-check and lint. Each effect was checked in the scene dev page (`gunship=1`, `fx=drop|airstrike`, keys `d`, `a`, `u`). Not yet played through in a real bus ride, and the HUD streak bar and announcer lines have not been seen or heard.

## VO2 Madness game mode (2026-11-13)
- A second crew game beside Zombie Bus, picked by the host in the lobby (`game` message, `Room.game`). No bus and no horde: the riders race along the road as a pack, and the whole pack gets the same mystery intervals. Rules are in `modes/vo2_madness.py`, numbers in `config.py` (`MAD_*`).
- Workout shape: a 45 to 80% warm-up ramp (about 15% of the session, 5 to 10 min), then rounds, then a cool-down (about 10%). Each round is a recovery at 50% followed by one card from the deck, so work to recovery stays near 1:1. The deck: Three-Minute Monster (3 min at 110%), Two-Minute Terror (2 min at 115%), Rocket Minute (1 min at 125%), 30/30 Mayhem (4 x 30 s at 125% / 30 s at 55%), 40/20 Meltdown (4 x 40 s at 120% / 20 s at 50%), Ramp of Doom (95 to 125% over 2.5 min), Stairway to Hell (3 x 1 min at 105, 112, 120%) and Mystery Sprint (15 s all out). The same card never comes twice in a row. Tiers scale the targets as usual and the 150% ERG cap still applies.
- Mystery box: the plan is dealt on the server and reshuffled when the ride starts, so the lobby never shows the real order. In the ride the timeline is hidden and the next interval reads as a mystery box until 10 s before it starts, when the box spins and lands on the name. The client still holds the whole plan (it needs it to pre-schedule ERG), so the secrecy is on the honour system.
- Scoring is all about effort. During intervals each rider scores points per second by heart-rate zone (0, 1, 2, 4, 7 for under 60, 70, 80, 90% of max, then over), multiplied by their compliance so soft-pedalling with a high heart rate does not pay. Riders with no strap or no max heart rate score a flat 3 per second times compliance. Nothing scores in recovery. Sprints pay by grade (0, 20, 40, 70, 100).
- The crown: whoever holds the highest share of their max heart rate (at least 75%) wears it, earns 1 extra point a second and leaves a trail of fire. A challenger must beat the holder by 2 points of max for 4 s. Seconds over 90% count as "in the red". A rider under 70% for 20 s while the rest average over 82% gets called out as a sandbagger.
- Twists: 60% of rounds come with one. Double Points, Redline Bonus (+5 a second over 90%), Hive Mind (x1.5 when the pack's cadence is within 10 rpm), Underdog (last place x3) and Leader Tax (first place x0.5).
- On screen: riders spread front to back by points (leader ahead, medal on the leader, crown over the hardest trier), heart-rate chips, reddening and steam as before, a left panel with the box, the twist and the effort leaderboard. The camera sits lower and closer than on the bus.
- End of ride: awards are Maddest (points), Red Liner (time over 90%), Crown Hog and PR Hunter. Madness results are not written to the season ladder, whose scores are bus scores. The Discord summary has its own wording.
- The bus-only messages (shop, difficulty, mutator) do nothing in this mode. The tick's `bus` object keeps its bus fields with neutral values and adds `mad`.
- Checked in a real ride against the simulated trainer and two bots on a 15x server clock (lobby switch, warm-up, recover, box, GO, crown, lead change, leaderboard), and in the scene dev page (`mad=1`). The results screen for this mode, the TV view and a full-length ride at real speed have not been looked at. At 15x the box spin had no time to land before GO.

## Zwift Click v1 and virtual gears (13/11/2026)
- Protocol sources (facts only, no code copied): [jat255/zwift_click_handling](https://github.com/jat255/zwift_click_handling) (MIT) and [OpenBikeControl/bikecontrol](https://github.com/OpenBikeControl/bikecontrol), formerly jonasbark/swiftcontrol. Both trace back to ajchellew/zwiftplay.
- Click v1: service `00000001-19ca-4651-86e5-fa29dcdd09d1`; notifications on Async `…0002` and Sync TX `…0004` (indicate); write the 6 bytes `RideOn` (`52 69 64 65 4f 6e`) to Sync RX `…0003` to start the unencrypted mode. The Click answers `RideOn 01 03` plus a key, which we ignore. Button messages are `0x37` then protobuf fields 1 (plus) and 2 (minus), value 0 pressed and 1 released; a missing field counts as pressed (protobuf default). Battery `0x19` and empty `0x15` messages are ignored. A press is a released-to-pressed change on one button; repeats and releases give nothing.
- Pairing filters on name prefix "Zwift Click" or the Zwift service. The handshake is sent without response if the characteristic allows it (bikecontrol does), else with response (jat255). Reconnect uses the heart-rate strap's backoff. Every raw notification is logged as `[Click]`. The driver never touches the trainer.
- Plus is shift up (harder), minus is shift down.
- Gears: 24 ratios from 0.75 to 5.49 in `CFG.gears`, spaced like Zwift's published spread (from memory of community tables, not checked against Zwift). Start in gear 12 (2.40), which is close to the default physical ratio 34/14 = 2.43, so the middle gear feels like the real bike on the flat.
- Gear model: virtual speed = cadence × gear ratio × 2.1 m; real wheel speed = cadence × chainring / 14 × 2.1 m. Using `core/physics.ts` `resistance`, bisect for the gradient at which the real speed costs the same power as the virtual speed on the road gradient. Below 20 rpm the road gradient is sent unchanged. The result is clamped to the mode's range (free ride −10 to 20%, Slope −5 to 15%). This assumes the trainer's own sim model (mass, Crr, CdA) is close to ours; it will not be exact.
- Free ride: the "road gradient" fed to the gear maths is the difficulty-scaled one, so the difficulty slider still works. Game speed is from power, as before. Gear does not persist between rides.
- Slope mode: the gear-adjusted gradient is resent when it moves by more than 0.2% (the free-ride step), so cadence wobble does not flood the trainer. All writes go through `ergController.setGrade`.
- Keys: `Z` down, `X` up, in free ride and Slope mode. Both were free in every handler (solo keys, free ride, simulated trainer, main, scene dev, spectator).
- Click in solo ERG mode: ±1% intensity; Resistance mode: ±1 level step (5%). Zombie Bus, FTP tests and the spectator view ignore it.
- Chainring (20 to 60 teeth, default 34) is a per-device setting on the Settings screen.
- Not confirmed from the references: whether every Click v1 firmware advertises the Zwift service UUID (we also filter on the name), and whether the trainer feels right with our physics. Needs a real Click and trainer.

## Bundled route packs (2026-11-13)
- The owner approved OpenStreetMap data (ODbL) for routes on 2026-11-13. Heights are NASA SRTM (public domain) through the Open Topo Data API. Both are credited on the route screen and in `CREDITS.md`.
- `scripts/routes/build_routes.py` makes the packs: it downloads the public roads around a route's waypoints (`scripts/routes/routes.json`), takes the shortest road path through them, and samples heights every 30 m. One-way streets are ignored, and service roads, tracks and cycleways are left out so the path stays on the road riders use.
- SRTM is noisy on roads cut into hillsides: raw, it overstated the alpine climbs by 20 to 40% and put 185 m of climbing on Beach Road. Heights are averaged over 300 m along the road before the pack is written, widened (up to 800 m) only when the climb is still more than 10% over the published figure. Mount Hotham needed 600 m. This flattens short pinches: expect a real 15% ramp to read a few per cent lower.
- A pack is refused when its length is more than 5% or its climb more than 10% from the published figures. Results: Ben Lomond 17.8 km / 1075 m (published 17.8 / 1083), Mount Buffalo 21.0 / 1069 (20.9 / 1013), Falls Creek 29.9 / 1335 (29.9 / 1253), Mount Hotham 27.5 / 1402 (28.9 / 1279), kunanyi from Fern Tree 11.6 / 820 (11.1 / 822).
- Exceptions, all shipped: Willunga Hill reads 202 m against 225 m (3 km at the Tour Down Under's 7.5%), 10.2% under, written with `--force`. Mount Coot-tha (12.1 km, 303 m, from the Botanic Gardens and round Sir Samuel Griffith Drive) and Akuna Bay and West Head (51.1 km, 856 m) have no published figure for the variant used, and Great Ocean Road (45.1 km, 438 m) and Beach Road (22.7 km, 90 m) have no published climb, so those numbers are unchecked.
- Packs hold the centreline only (lat, lon, height every 10 m). The height grid and map features in the plan arrive with Phases B and C.
- `client/tests/routePacks.test.ts` loads every pack through the real loader and compares it with its card, so a broken pack or path fails the suite.

## Route packs: terrain grid (2026-11-13)
- Each pack now carries a `terrain` block for the 3D world: SRTM heights on a 60 m grid, in whole metres, for every cell within 900 m of the road, stored row by row as runs of known cells. The grid's own corner (`lat0`, `lon0`) is the origin of the local-metre projection the scene uses. See `scripts/routes/terrain.py` for the exact layout.
- Heights come straight from SRTM 1-arc-second tiles (public domain) on the AWS open terrain tile bucket, read with the standard library. Sea and data voids are 0 m.
- The grid is not smoothed: the road's own heights win near the road (the scene cuts the ground to the road), so the two only need to agree roughly. On Mount Coot-tha the nearest grid cell is within 10 m of the road height everywhere and the median difference is 0.

## 2026-11-13 Free ride 3D road world (Phase B): road and terrain
- New code lives in `client/src/scene/route/`. It reuses the bus scene's sky, moods, riders, bike, quality levels and debug panel, and adds a `route` mood (blue sky, fog out to 1100 m). The world is fed a generic list of riders (`RoadState`), so later road games can use it unchanged.
- Projection is flat local metres around the terrain grid's origin (or the route's first point): east = dlon x 111320 x cos(lat0), north = dlat x 110540. Scene x is east, z is south, y is height in metres.
- Pack coordinates are rounded to about 1 m, which makes the heading jitter, so the centreline gets two 1-2-1 smoothing passes before a Catmull-Rom curve is run through it. Tight hairpins come out a metre or two wider than the data.
- Short out-and-back spurs are cut out of the drawn line (a stretch that returns to within 6 m of itself inside 80 m of road). Mount Coot-tha has one at 1.3 km where the route builder snapped a waypoint onto a side road. The 15 points either side are respaced to absorb the lost distance, so the rider moves about 15% slower on screen for 300 m rather than stalling. The ride's distance and gradient are untouched. The pack itself still has the spur; fixing the builder would be better.
- Banking: curvature x 1.2 radians, clamped to 0.1 rad (about 6 degrees). Riders lean by speed squared x curvature / g on top, clamped to 0.45 rad.
- Road: 6.2 m of tarmac, 1.2 m gravel verges, white edge lines and a white dashed centre line (4 m on, 8 m off), one row of triangles every 4 m. Chunks are 200 m; they are built up to 800 m ahead at medium quality (350, 500, 1000 for potato, low, high) and kept 150 m behind. Any road within 600 m as the crow flies is also built, so the other legs of a switchback are there when you look across. One chunk is built per frame (about 3 to 5 ms each in software rendering).
- The road carries straight on for 200 m before the start and 400 m after the finish so the world does not end at a cliff.
- Ground height: the pack's 60 m grid, sampled bilinearly, trusted fully three cells in from its edge and fading to made-up ground at the edge. Made-up ground (imported GPX, or beyond the grid) is the heights of nearby road spread sideways, weighted towards the nearest, plus value noise up to 70 m scaled down on flat routes.
- Cut and fill: the ground is level with the road for 7.5 m from the centreline and blends back to natural ground over the next 45 m. Each nearby stretch of road gets its own bench; between two stacked legs the nearer one wins, so the bank between them is steep. This is weighted over all centreline segments within range from a spatial hash, not just the nearest one.
- Terrain mesh: rows of vertices running out from the road every 4 m, at 8, 12, 18, 27, 40, 60 and 90 m, then every 40 m (closer round bends) out to 650 m. A row stops where its ground is nearer to some other bit of road, overlaps by 2.5 m and drops a 30 m skirt, so strips from two legs meet instead of crossing. This replaced the planned "fine strip plus square tiles": one mechanism, no seams between tile sizes, and vertices land exactly on the verge. Known flaw: thin cracks and dark skirt lines can show where two strips meet on steep ground, and beyond about 600 m there can be holes where the land belongs to road that is not built.
- Colours: grass, bush and dry grass from noise, rock on faces steeper than about 37 degrees, snow in patches above 1650 m (Hotham's top; Falls Creek gets a little). All in `VIS.route` and the debug panel; ground colours are baked per chunk, so the panel rebuilds the chunks.
- Skyline: a ring 1500 m out, as tall as the land looks from the rider in each direction (sampled at three distances), redrawn every 80 m and eased. Beyond the pack's grid that land is made up.
- Floating origin: everything in route metres hangs off one group that is shifted whenever the rider gets 300 m from the origin; chunk vertices are stored relative to their own anchor.
- Imported routes are saved without terrain (`routeStore.ts` strips it).

## 2026-11-13 Free ride Phase C: painted ground and side streets
- Data contract: `terrain.cover` (one letter per grid cell: g grass, f forest, b bush, s scrub, u built-up, p park, a sand, r rock, w water) and a top-level `features` object (`roads`, `buildings`, `pois`) in the grid's local metres. Both are carried pack to route to scene like terrain, every part is optional, and imported routes are stored without them. Features are ignored when there is no height grid, because they share its origin.
- The real map data was not in the packs when this was built. Everything was checked against made-up data from the harness (`fixture=1`: noisy zones of each cover class with lakes, a side street every 250 m with houses, a peak, lookout, towers and cafe) and against the no-data guesses.
- No cover data: ground within 2 m above sea level is sand, below it water; otherwise bush with patches of forest (26% of the area) and grassy clearings (20%) from slow noise; heath above the treeline (1,500 m, 1,200 m when the route's region is TAS, wobbling by up to 80 m) and rock 200 m above that. Mapped grass above the treeline is treated as unmapped and guessed the same way. Ground below 0.5 m is always water.
- Cover boundaries wander by up to 0.35 of a grid cell with 28 m noise. Each face takes two samples with different noise and is a half-way colour where they disagree. Each class has its own colour drifting towards a neighbour; steep faces are still rock and high ground still gets snow.
- Water: the bed is painted dark blue-green and a separate level sheet sits 0.12 m above each wet face's mean height, in a shinier material so it catches the sun. On sloping "water" (a mapped river on a hill) the sheets step.
- The terrain vertices of a chunk are now kept in a `Patch` per side while the chunk is built, so streets and props read the height of the drawn triangles rather than the smooth ground. Away from the patch (another leg's ground) the smooth height is used.

## 2026-11-13 Free ride Phase D: physics and the trainer's look-ahead
- Momentum was already there (speed is integrated from force over mass), so nothing changed; a test now proves speed gained in a dip carries up the other side. No wheel inertia: not worth a number.
- Bends have a speed: `sqrt(grip / curvature)` with grip 7 m/s² (about 0.7 g, a confident club rider) and a floor of 5.5 m/s (20 km/h) so the tightest hairpin never stops anyone. A 30 m radius bend allows 52 km/h, an 8 m hairpin 27 km/h. Curvature comes from the same smoothed centreline the 3D world draws (`RoadPath`), built once more for the ride so the flat layout rides the same.
- Braking is automatic and early: each tick the road ahead is checked every 5 m out to the stopping distance, and the allowed speed is whatever lets the rider slow at 2.5 m/s² to each bend's speed. The brakes never bite harder than 4 m/s². It applies everywhere, not only downhill, but only matters when fast. Riding all 16 bundled routes at 220 W costs at most 0.9% more time (Akuna Bay, 29 s on the brakes in 107 min); most routes lose nothing. There is no brake light or HUD note: it should just feel like a rider who knows the road.
- Wind (Settings, Free ride wind: Off, Breeze 3 m/s, Blowy 6 m/s; off by default) drags on air speed, so it is a headwind one way and a tailwind the other. Where it blows from is fixed for a route and a day (hashed from both). The sky scales it: dawn 0.6, golden hour 0.8, dusk 0.7, mist 0.4, a shower 1.4; and it swells and drops 15% over 23 s. With wind on, the stats drawer shows "Head 11 km/h" or "Tail 6 km/h". Ghost riders (`?ghosts=N`) ignore wind and bends.
- The gradient sent to the trainer is the road 1.5 s of travel ahead (at most 30 m), then difficulty and virtual gear as before, through the same `ErgController.setGrade` and the same -10% to +20% clamp. Speed, the HUD gradient and the recorded climb still use the road under the wheel. 1.5 s is a guess at a smart trainer's lag; `CFG.freeRide.lead` if it arrives early or late on the real thing. All of it lives in one pure function (`trainerTarget` in `modes/freeRide/feel.ts`), tested to stay in range and to produce only gradient writes.
- Numbers: `CFG.freeRide.corner`, `.wind`, `.lead`, `.cheer`.

## 2026-11-13 Free ride Phase D: camera, rider and sound
- Nobody has watched these move or heard them: the author works from still screenshots at a few frames a second and from unit tests. Every amount is a guess meant to be tuned by the owner in the debug panel (`g`, "feel") or in `CFG.sound.ride`.
- Camera extras (`scene/route/feelVis.ts`, `VIS.route.feel.cam`), all small on purpose: the view widens 6 degrees by 18 m/s (was 5, inline); a two-tone 8 Hz shake of at most 2.8 cm and 0.003 rad, only above 11 m/s on at least 4% downhill, full at 19 m/s (there is no road-surface data, so "rough" means fast and downhill); behind a tucked rider the camera comes 22% closer and 0.9 m lower; it rolls 30% of the rider's lean into a bend, at most 0.07 rad (4 degrees); and the part of the aim that follows the gradient is eased on its own at 2/s so a ramp tips the view gently. Lean into bends was already there (now capped by `feel.rider.maxLean`). Poses blend at 3/s.

- `scene/riders.ts` lost its name tag to `scene/riderTag.ts` to stay under 300 lines; bus and VO2 riders pose exactly as before.
- Sound is all WebAudio synthesis, no files, so nothing to license (`ui/rideSound.ts`, maths in `core/soundMix.ts`). Freehub: a 12 ms click ringing at 3.4 kHz, 18 clicks a wheel turn, capped at 45 a second, only at zero watts above 0.8 m/s. Wind: band-passed noise from 3 m/s, full at 20 m/s through the air (so a headwind is louder), pitch rising with speed. Tyres: low-passed noise growing with the square root of speed. Gear change: a 150 Hz thunk and two clicks 50 ms apart, only when the gear actually changes.
- The place: land cover beside the rider picks birdsong (random phrases of 2 to 5 sliding notes at 2.2 to 4.2 kHz), cicadas (4.8 kHz noise chopped at 46 Hz), a murmur of traffic in built-up cover and parks, surf and gulls when sand or water is within 150 m either side and the rider is below 60 m (so a dam in the hills is not the sea), and mountain wind fading in from 900 m to 1,500 m, which takes the cicadas and most birds away. The sky scales it: a dawn chorus (birds 1.6 times), no cicadas at dawn, in mist or in a shower, more at golden hour. The flat (charts) layout has no world to ask, so it plays bush everywhere.
- Loudness per category in `CFG.sound.ride.level`, all under the existing volume setting, plus a "Free ride sounds" switch (on by default). Sound starts with the ride, which only starts from a click, and the context is resumed if the browser had it suspended. Levels were set by measuring the output in headless Chromium, not by ear: about 0.007 RMS for bush at a standstill, 0.03 pedalling at 36 km/h, 0.08 coasting at 65 km/h, 0.5 peak for a gear clunk.
- The harness got `power=0`, `effort=`, `cheer=1`, `calm=1` and `sound=1` so each pose and sound can be looked at or listened to on its own.
- Found while taking the lap screenshots: at the start of every lap the chase camera sat on a straight line carried back from the start instead of on the end of the circuit, so it cut across the grass for the first few metres. On a route marked `loop` the camera now wraps its own spot and its aim point round the lap. The aim point is also held within 4 m of where it should be, as the camera already was, so a very slow frame rate cannot leave it staring at the tarmac. The ground where the two ends of a circuit meet still overlaps untidily; that is scenery, not touched here.


## 2026-11-14 Free ride Phase E: ride together

- A group ride is a new kind of room (`POST /api/rooms {kind: 'road'}`), not a bus game: `RoadRoom` subclasses the bus `Room` for its sockets, lobby broadcast, spectators, host hand-over and snapshots, and replaces the plan and bus with a `RoadSim`. Every lobby message now says its `kind`, and a client that joined the wrong sort of room by code hands over to the right flow, so the one "join by code" box and the open-rooms list work for both.
- Three layers so road games can be added without touching the first two: physics and geometry (`road_physics.py`, `road_route.py`), the road (`modes/road.py`: `RoadSim` and `Mover`), and the rules (`Rules`, with `place`, `tune`, `after`, `state`, `over`, `results`). The plain ride is the base `Rules`. See `docs/road-games.md`. No game is built.
- Same physics both sides: the server's `step_speed`, `resistance`, `corner_speed`, `speed_limit`, gradient resampling and centreline curvature are ports of the client's, and `content/tests/road-vectors.json` (generated by `client/tests/roadVectors.test.ts` with `ROAD_VECTORS=write`) is checked by vitest and pytest: every bundled route's distance, 40 gradients, curvatures and bend limits, plus grids of physics calls. The server's physics numbers are copied into `config.py` as `ROAD_*`; the vectors fail if either side changes one.
- The server ticks and broadcasts road rooms at 4 Hz (the bus stays at 2 Hz). Clients send power, cadence and heart rate every 0.5 s in the bus's `data` message. No data for 3 s reads as zero power.
- The countdown is 10 s (`ROAD_COUNTDOWN_S`; the bus has 5) because the road world takes a few seconds to load and the start is a line-up worth seeing. The ride clock on the HUD is the server's.
- The flat (charts) layout works in a group ride and shows everyone on its map, but has no rider list or drafting chip; those are on the 3D HUD only.
- No wind in a group ride (the server has no sky), no pause and no auto-pause: a rider who stops pedalling coasts to a stop and the ride goes on. `?fast=` is ignored.
- My own rider is predicted locally with the same physics and eased onto the server's position: 60% of the gap per second, and taken whole past 40 m (a rejoin or late join). Other riders are carried on at their last speed between ticks (at most 1 s). Not tried over a slow network.
- Weight comes from the rider's stored profile on the server (`weightKg`, 75 kg when unset), read at the start and at a late join.
- Drafting (invented, tunable in `config.py` `DRAFT_*`, mirrored in `CFG.road.draft`): 27% less CdA within 2 m of a wheel, fading in a straight line to nothing at 10 m; each extra rider ahead within 20 m adds 15% of that; capped at 40%. Nobody drafts a rider going slower than 3 m/s. Riders are points on a line and pass through each other: there is no blocking, so two equal riders swap turns on their own as the one behind gains and then loses the draft.
- The HUD chip (bottom right, beside the gear) shows "Drafting −N W" from 5 W up, where N is the aerodynamic power the saving is worth at the current speed. The client works the draft out itself from the latest positions with the same sum (so the chip and the speed answer at once); the server's figure is what moves the rider.
- Keep-together (invented, `BAND_*`): distance covered is scaled by how far the rider is from the median of the riders who are moving or pedalling; nothing within 15 m, growing to ±20% at 150 m. It scales distance, never the physics speed or the gradient, so the trainer feels nothing. A stopped rider is left out of the median and is not towed. On by default for a social ride, off for a race; the host can flip it either way. The chip shows "Tow +N%" or "Held −N%".
- Lanes: the server ranks riders front to back (one bubble pass a tick with a metre of slack, so two riders side by side do not flicker) and puts them alternately 2.3 m and 1.1 m left of the centre line. The scene eases a rider across when their lane changes.
- Late join: on the road 5 m behind the last rider still moving, at that rider's speed (at the back of the group, not back at the start, so a late mate is in the ride at once). Their time and distance count from where they joined, they are marked "joined late" and ranked after everyone who rode the whole way.
- Finish: each rider finishes on reaching the distance (time interpolated inside the tick). The ride ends when every human still connected has finished, or 10 minutes after the first finisher, or after 10 minutes with no human connected, or when the host ends it. Bots do not hold the ride open; a bot still out shows the distance it rode.
- Results per rider: place, time, average power (work over the time on the road), W/kg and average watts saved by drafting. A group ride is saved in history as a crew ride. No scrap, awards or ladder entry yet.
- A rider who presses End mid-ride gets their own summary and leaves the room; the room carries on.
- Places on the road: 8 (`ROAD_MAX_RIDERS`). A person joining a full lobby takes a bot's place.
- Bots (invented, `ROAD_BOTS`, `ROAD_BOT_X`): Diesel Dave holds 80% of FTP; Surging Shaz 70% with a 20 s surge at 120% every 90 s; Mountain Goat Mick 72%, 100% on anything 3% or steeper; Sprinter Trev 72%, then 220% for the last 300 m; Wheelsucker Wozza 84% in the wind and 66% on a wheel. Power wobbles ±4%. Each bot's FTP is the humans' median W/kg times the bot's own weight (58 to 88 kg) and a fitness of 0.95 to 1.05, so the bots ride with whoever is in the room; 3.0 W/kg if there is nobody to copy. Their one-liners show in the lobby.
- Road rooms survive a server restart the same way the bus does (snapshot every 5 s, paused until a rider rejoins). Bot surge timing restarts from the ride clock, so nothing is lost.
- TV view: `?watch=CODE` (or Watch on the room card) on a road room shows the road world following the leader, the rider list with gaps, and the results. Arrow keys follow the next or previous rider, L goes back to the leader.
- `freeRide.ts` lost its key map to `keys.ts` to stay near 300 lines; solo free ride runs the same loop with no link and behaves as before (same tests, same numbers).
- The one ERG write in a road ride is the existing fail-safe when the ride stops (50% of FTP, then release), as in every other mode. During the ride the trainer only gets `setGrade`; `tests/roadLink.test.ts` and `test_road_multiplayer.py` check both ends.
- Not built: imported GPX routes in a group ride (the server only has the bundled packs), a postcard for road rides, Discord post, scrap for finishing.

## 2026-11-14 Free ride Phase F: reasons to come back

### Segments
- Every route gets its segments worked out from the route itself (`core/segments.ts`), nothing hand-made: each sustained climb (the same detection as the HUD's climb panel), one sprint, and the whole route. On a circuit the whole route is "one lap" instead, and a lap ride of any length has the same segments as its lap; a ride of N laps is not a segment of its own.
- A climb is named after a named peak or lookout within 400 m of its top when the pack has one (`CFG.segments.poiM`), with a number if two climbs share it; otherwise "<route> · climb N" in road order.
- The sprint is 300 m (`sprint.lenM`): the last stretch on the route (so nearest the finish, or the line on a circuit) with no gradient past 2% either way; when a route has none, the flattest 300 m. Routes under 600 m get no sprint.
- Segment ids are `<route id>:<kind>:<start rounded to 100 m>` (`idRoundM`), so they survive a session and a small rebuild of a pack. A rebuild that moves a climb's start by more than that makes a new segment and orphans the old efforts.
- Timing uses the ride clock (moving time in a solo ride, so a pause does not count; the server's clock in a group ride) and is taken at the line, not at the 200 ms tick. Average power is work over the time. With `?fast=N` times are game time.
- Live splits are against the pace of the best effort itself: every effort keeps a trace (metres from the segment's start every 5 s, `CFG.segments.traceS`), and the split is your time so far minus the time the best took to reach the same spot. So a split agrees with the ghost up the road. A best with no trace (none exist yet) shows its time to beat but no live split.
- "Crew" in a split is the fastest rider other than you. Taking the crew best means beating that time with a PR you had not already beaten it with.
- The panel: on a climb the segment is a column of the existing climb panel (name, time, splits); a sprint gets a small panel in the same place, from 100 m out. The whole route or lap split is a row in the stats drawer ("This ride" / "This lap"), not a panel. The flat (charts) layout gets the toasts and results but no panel.
- Results: a toast for every finished climb and sprint; a PR or first go gets the three-note jingle and holds 7 s (`CFG.hud.toastBigS`), a crew best the five-note one, and both are spoken when the announcer is on. A lap that is not a PR only gets the usual lap toast. Lines are in `fun/segmentLines.ts`, picked from the time so the same result always says the same thing.
- While a fanfare toast is up, ordinary toasts (the commentator, lap toasts) are not shown, so a PR is not talked over the moment it lands. The commentator is still heard.
- Efforts are saved as they are finished (not at the end of the ride), so a crash keeps them. A late join or rejoin in a group ride (a jump of more than 40 m) drops any go under way and starts none.
- Server: one new table, `efforts` (rider, route, segment, secs, watts, wkg, date, ride_date, trace), made with `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS` and two indexes; nothing existing is altered. An effort is unique by rider, segment and finish time, so sending the same one twice stores it once.
- The "ride id" of an effort is looked up when asked for, from the ride's start time (`ride_date`), because efforts are sent as they are finished and the ride itself is only posted at the end. It is null for a ride that was never saved (under 30 s, or posted while offline).
- Leaderboards are each rider's best, fastest first, or by W/kg (`by=wkg`: each rider's highest W/kg effort, which need not be their fastest). Ten rows (`LEADERBOARD_ROWS`).
- Offline: efforts go to a queue in localStorage beside this device's copy of the rider's bests (`core/efforts.ts`); the queue is sent after every effort and at login, and holds at most 200 (`CFG.segments.queueMax`). Rides themselves still have no offline queue (they never had one), so an offline ride keeps its segment times and PRs but not its history entry.
- Limits (server `config.py`): an effort under 24 h, a trace of at most 6,000 points, 500 efforts a post.

### Ghosts
- A ghost is a best effort over the whole route (one lap, on a circuit) ridden again. Whole-route and lap efforts keep a trace: metres from the start every 5 s (`CFG.segments.traceS`), so a two-hour ride is about 1,400 small numbers. The server keeps the trace only for each rider's best on a segment; this device keeps its own best's trace in localStorage so the ghost works offline.
- On a lap ride the ghost rides its best lap over and over from the start of the ride, so by lap 5 it is five best laps up the road. It is not restarted at the line each lap.
- The ghost starts when the ride clock starts and runs on the ride clock, so it stops when a solo ride is paused.
- Setup screen: once a route has a best, the start bar shows "◌ Your PR 12:34" ticked by default, and a list of mates who have a best on it (from the route's leaderboard) to pick one from. Up to two ghosts ride: yours and one mate's. A group ride brings your own PR ghost along automatically, seen only by you; there is no mate picker in the lobby.
- Ghosts are only ever positions that are drawn and listed: they are not sent to the server, are not in the drafting sum, and do not count for the keep-together median. The ride loop adds them to the drawn and listed riders after the server's riders are read, and nothing reads them back.
- Drawn see-through and pale (`VIS.route.ghost`: 40% solid, 55% towards white, in the debug panel under "Ghost riders"), 1.2 m and 2.3 m right of the rider's lane so they do not sit inside the rider. In the rider list a ghost has a ring mark (◌), italics, no W/kg, and the usual gap.
- Not looked at in motion: the ghost was checked in still screenshots only.

### FIT export with position
- A free or group ride now records metres along the route at each recorded second (`dist` in the saved ride, beside `power`) and which route and how many laps (`route`). The FIT file is built at download time by reading the position, height and distance for each second off the route, so nothing bulky is stored. Speed is the distance covered in that second.
- This needs the route on the device at download time: always true for bundled routes, true for an imported GPX only while it is still in this browser's "My routes". Without the route (or for a route with no coordinates) the file is the same power-only file as before.
- With a track the session's sub-sport is virtual activity (58) instead of indoor cycling (6), which is what Strava and intervals.icu expect for a ride with a map that was ridden indoors, and the session gets total distance, total ascent and average speed.
- Lap rides get one lap message per lap (the last may be a part lap); other rides with a track get one lap for the whole ride. Lap messages carry a message index and the lap's distance.
- A ride without positions is byte-for-byte what it was (`tests/fitTrack.test.ts` holds the old encoder's length and checksum for a sample ride).
- Not uploaded anywhere: the files were decoded by our own test reader only. Whether Strava draws the map is for the owner to try.

### Route passport
- Rider tab, new "Passport" sub-tab: the map, a table of every bundled route (times ridden, best time, metres climbed) and the passport badges.
- The map is drawn in code from about 85 hand-typed longitude and latitude corners (`core/ausMap.ts`): mainland, Tasmania, six border lines and state labels. No ACT, no islands, gulfs mostly ironed out. It is a plain degrees projection squeezed 0.89 east-west.
- Route dots sit where the route starts (`ROUTE_AT`, checked against the packs by a test, so a new route without a dot fails the suite). Dots closer than 15 map units (`CFG.passport.dotGap`) are pushed apart with a thin line back to the real spot, because Brisbane has five routes on top of each other. Dots are numbered to match the table and fill in once the route has been finished.
- The passport lives in the profile (`passport[routeId] = {n, bestS, vertM, first}`), so it syncs with the profile and needs no new server code. It is stamped at the end of every free or group ride: each finished whole-route effort (or lap, on a circuit) is a time ridden; best time is the best of those (best lap on a circuit); climbing counts whether or not the ride was finished. Imported GPX routes are stamped too but are not on the map.
- Rides from before Phase F are not in the passport: old rides did not record their route.
- Badges, in the existing badge list (ids start `pp`): First Stamp (1 route), Frequent Flyer (5 routes), Interstate Incident (a route in every state that has one), All of Brisbane (every route starting within 25 km of the city: Coot-tha loop, Coot-tha Back, Mondo, Full Gas Friday, Nundah crit), Grand Tour (every route), Regular (5 finishes of one route) and Part of the furniture (20), and three climbing tiers on a single route: Hill Regular 2,000 m, Base Camp 4,424 m (half an Everest), Everested 8,848 m. The repeat and climbing badges are named after the route they were earned on ("Everested Coot-tha"). All numbers in `CFG.passport`.

### Weekly featured route and climbing target
- The route of the week is picked from the week number alone (weeks start at midnight on Monday by the device's clock, so mates in one time zone agree; the older streak counter's week starts on Sunday UTC and was left alone), so every rider sees the same one with no server: a hash of the week over the routes sorted by id, stepping on one if it lands on last week's. It shows in a "This week" card at the top of the free-ride setup (with "Pick it") and of the Passport, and its route card is outlined.
- Finishing it (a lap of it, on a circuit) pays 30 scrap (`CFG.weekly.featuredScrap`), solo or in a group ride.
- The weekly climb is personal: 1.1 times the average of the last 4 weeks in which the rider climbed anything in free ride, to the nearest 50 m, between 300 and 6,000 m; 600 m for someone with no history. It only looks at earlier weeks, so it does not move during the week. Reaching it pays 50 scrap.
- Both are paid at the end of a ride through the same profile update that banks bus scrap, and each at most once a week: the week's log in the profile (`weeks[week] = {vertM, paid}`) remembers what was paid, so finishing twice pays once. The profile keeps five weeks of these.
- Only free-ride and group-ride climbing counts; workouts and the bus have no hills.

### Ride history
- A free or group ride's row in History shows a small line map of its route, the route name, the distance and "free ride" or "group ride". The ride's detail gets a route card (the map with the part ridden drawn brighter and the start marked, distance, climbing, whether it was finished) and the same segments card as the end-of-ride summary.
- The map is an SVG line drawn from the bundled pack (or the imported route if this browser still has it), not Leaflet: no tiles, no network, and cheap enough for a list. A ride on a route this device no longer has shows no map.
- The list endpoint gained `route` (the route id) and `distance`; both are null for other rides and for rides from before Phase F.

### Closing Phase F gaps

Pick a route, then a segment (the whole route or lap first), then time or W/kg. No new endpoint: it reads `/api/segments/{id}/leaderboard`. The server sends ten rows, which covers the crew; "You are 2nd of 5" counts the rows shown.

- In a ride the stats drawer shows "Top three" for the climb or sprint in play (from its lead-in to its end) and for the whole route or lap otherwise. A PR set during the ride is folded in on the device, as "You". Hidden when nobody has a time.

It is built from the same pieces as the 3D HUD (`ui/segPanel.ts`, `ui/segBoard.ts`, `ui/riderList.ts`) and hides itself when it has nothing to say.

No behaviour change.

### Pre-ride flyover and loading card
- Every free or group ride opens with a card (`ui/rideIntro.ts`): route name, distance, climb, a small profile and one dry line about the place. It covers the screen while the world loads; once the first frame of the stretch is drawn it becomes a caption in the lower left and the camera sweeps the stretch (`FlyShot` in `scene/route/camShots.ts`), then cuts to the chase camera. The HUD and the "start pedalling" overlay stay hidden until it is over, and the ride clock cannot start before then.
- What it shows (`core/flyover.ts`, unit-tested): the tightest pair of hairpins on the main climb if two are within 500 m of each other; else the last 600 m to the top of the main climb (the biggest by gain, from 60 m); else the steepest 600 m if it averages 2.5%; else the middle of the route. The coast is not detected: on the two coast routes the whole road is the coast, so any stretch does.
- Numbers (`CFG.intro`, `VIS.route.fly` in the debug panel): 7 s over 600 m of road, from 42 m up and 34 m out to the right down to 7 m up and 5 m out, trailing the point it is about by 30 m and looking 70 m past it. A slow frame moves the sweep on by at most 0.5 s, so a slow device sees all of it, only slower (in the software renderer it runs well over 7 s).
- Any key or tap skips it; the key is swallowed so it does not also pause or shift. `?intro=0` leaves it out (dev aid). In the 2D layout, or with no WebGL, the card alone shows for 3.5 s.
- Group rides: the flyover runs inside the server's 10 s countdown and the camera is back behind the rider with 3 s to go (`CFG.intro.settleS`). A late joiner gets no flyover.
- The lines are in `fun/routeLines.ts`, one per bundled route, written to be safe: no dates, records or history. The only named feature is Jacob's Ladder on Ben Lomond (already named in the plan). An imported GPX gets one of three spare lines. A test fails if a bundled route has no line.
- The camera hook is general: `RouteWorld.shot` is any `CamShot`; while one runs the world is built, lit and centred around what the shot looks at rather than the rider.

### Summit and finish moments
- Two moments a ride, each once (`modes/freeRide/moments.ts`): over the highest point of the route when the route has a climb worth one (the commentator's existing 60 m rule), and at the finish. A top within 300 m of the line (`CFG.moments.nearFinishM`) is left to the finish, so Willunga gets one moment, not two.
- Each moment: confetti and streamers thrown up over the rider (220 pieces, one in five a streamer, one instanced mesh, 5 s; `VIS.route.party` in the debug panel), one turn of the camera round the rider over 5 s that starts and ends where the chase camera sits (`OrbitShot`, `VIS.route.orbit`), the announcer's line (spoken when the announcer is on; `fun/momentLines.ts`, picked from the ride time), the three-note jingle at a summit and the five-note one at the finish, and a stats card for 7 s: time, average power, W/kg and PRs so far.
- Calm camera skips the turn of the camera (the confetti and the card stay).
- A solo ride now holds at the line for 6 s (`finishHoldS`) so the finish can be seen, then shows the results; nothing is recorded during the hold (the ride is paused, the trainer gets the paused gradient). With `?fast=N` the hold is 1.5 s. A group ride's finish moment fires when the server marks the rider finished; the results still arrive when the server ends the ride.
- The postcard: a still of the 3D view is taken 42% of the way round the turn (three-quarters on, from the front), or straight from the chase camera when there is no turn. The results screen of any free or group ride gets "Save postcard": a 1200 x 800 PNG with the still (the lookout one if there was a summit, else the finish; in the 2D layout the ride's power drawn as a range of hills), route name, date, distance, climb, time, average power, W/kg and PRs. The still lives in memory only: it is not stored with the ride, so a postcard cannot be made later from History.
- The crew-results postcard for the bus (`ui/resultsGroup.ts`) was left as it is: it is laid out around the bus's score and awards, so the free-ride one is its own small file (`ui/ridePostcard.ts`).
- Group rides: the results screen shows a podium (second, first, third, with faces and times) above the crew table. It is a drawn podium on the results screen, not riders standing on steps in the 3D world.
- Tidying to make room: the results screen's blocks table moved to `ui/resultsBlocks.ts`, the sun's set-up to `scene/route/sun.ts`.

### Settings
- All new settings are per device, in `core/settings.ts`, on the existing Settings screen.
- Units: metric or imperial. Storage and all maths stay metric; `core/units.ts` converts at the screen (`ui/units.ts` is the same with the rider's setting applied). Converted: the free-ride HUD and 2D layout (speed, distance, climb, wind, distance and gain to the top), the route cards, the weekly card, the loading card, the results tiles, the postcard, the passport, the group-ride tables, and the weight box in Rider settings (typed in pounds, stored in kilograms to 0.1 kg). Left in metric on purpose: short countdowns in metres ("Climb in 120 m", gaps to riders when stopped), the elevation chart's axis labels, the History list and ride detail, the bus and workout screens (they have no distances that matter), roadside signs in the 3D world (Australian roads are in kilometres), and W/kg.
- Camera distance: near, standard, far scale the chase camera's distance behind and height by 0.7, 1 and 1.5 (`CFG.camDist`, applied as `VIS.route.camDist`). It shares a settings row with the calm-camera switch.
- HUD size was already a setting; the same control is now also in the ride menu (the ☰ button), and the Settings row says so.
- Colour-blind colours: the existing "colour-blind" switch now also swaps the gradient colours (elevation strip, climb panel, gradient readout) to a second palette, `CFG.hud.gradeBandsCb`, taken from the Okabe-Ito set: sky blue for descents, grey for flat, then yellow, orange, vermilion, reddish purple and white for 15% and over. One switch rather than two: a rider who needs one palette needs the other. The gradient is always written as a number beside the colour. The 3D gradient signs were never colour-coded (dark plate, pale numerals), so they needed nothing.
- Reduced motion: Auto (the default) follows the device's `prefers-reduced-motion`; On and Off override it. When it applies: the calm camera is forced (no shake, lean, tuck or widening view), there is no flyover (the loading card alone, 3.5 s), no turn of the camera at a summit or the finish, no confetti, and the stats card does not bounce in. The postcard still is taken from the chase camera instead.
- Sound mix: three sliders under the master volume. "Ride sounds" (freehub, wind, tyres, gear clunks) and "Ambience" (birds, cicadas, surf, gulls, mountain wind, traffic) are two gain buses in `ui/rideSound.ts`; "Voice" scales the spoken announcer and commentator (bus rides too). The beeps and jingles stay on the master volume alone.

Always on in the route harness (`/route-dev.html`, also `window.readPerf()`); in a ride with `?perf=1`. It allocates nothing per frame.

- There is no clean "before" for Auto. The one reading taken before the fix below (Coot-tha, Auto, part-way through this work) was 0.4 fps with 662k triangles, still on High after 50 s. Earlier phases wrote down 2 to 6 fps for these routes.
- What was found, in the order it mattered:
  1. **Auto quality never stepped down when it was most needed.** The stepper was fed the frame time after it had been capped at 0.1 s, so at 0.4 fps it thought it was seeing 10 fps windows that took 50 real seconds each. It now gets the real frame time.
  2. **A drop in level did not thin what was already built.** Chunks built at High stayed until ridden past (133k triangles at "Low power"). A drop now rebuilds the chunks at once.
  3. **Edge smoothing (MSAA).** One trial with it off: 2.7 to 4.7 fps on Coot-tha at Low. It cannot be switched on a live renderer, so a world that starts at Low or below is made without it; Medium and High keep it. Auto remembers where it settled on this device (`cranky.gfx.auto`) and starts the next ride there, so a slow device gets this from its second ride on.
  4. **Plants are most of the triangles** (one trial with none: 74k to 18k triangles, 2.7 to 5.0 fps). Left alone at Low and up (the look is the point); the new level cuts them hard.
  5. **Loops built too much.** Road within 600 m as the crow flies was always built, which on the Nundah crit is the whole circuit. That reach is now scaled by level: 20% at Low power, 50% at Potato, 75% at Low, all of it at Medium and High.
- Step rule now (`VIS.quality`): 2 s windows; a window under 20 fps drops two levels at once; two windows under 40 fps drop one (as before); ten over 57 fps climb one (as before). From High to the floor is 4 s, where reaching Potato used to take 12 s.
- New level "Low power", below Potato: 60% resolution, no shadows, 200 m ahead (Potato 350), a tenth of the plants within 35 m of the road (Potato a quarter within 60 m), a fifth of the oddities and wildlife, no far terrain. It is in Settings, Graphics, and Auto can reach it. The bus scene uses the same level list: there it is only the lower resolution.
- Hidden tab: browsers already stop animation frames for a hidden tab; where one still delivers them the world now draws at most once a second (`VIS.route.hiddenFrameMs`). The ride itself (the 200 ms tick, the trainer) is untouched.
- Looked for and not changed, because nothing here could measure them: draw calls (40 to 90, already instanced per kind per chunk), sign canvas textures (made once per chunk build), per-frame allocations, transparency overdraw. Chunk builds are 100 to 200 ms each under this CPU cap and are the cause of the 1% lows; they were left on the main thread.
- Invented numbers to tune on real hardware: the 20 fps "very slow" line and the two-level drop, everything about Low power, and the `nearbyShare` table.

### Rides finished offline
- A finished ride (any mode, 30 s or longer) now goes into a queue on the device first (`core/rideQueue.ts`, localStorage, one queue per rider) and is sent from there; it leaves the queue when the server has taken it. What cannot be sent waits, and goes up at the next login and whenever the browser says the network is back (the same moments queued segment efforts are sent).
- The queue holds five rides (`CFG.rideQueueMax`): a ride carries its per-second arrays, roughly 100 kB an hour, and localStorage is small. If storage is full the oldest queued rides are dropped to make room for the new one. A ride the server refuses outright (a 4xx) is dropped; a server error or no network keeps it.
- Idempotent on the server: `POST /api/rides` ignores a ride the rider already has with the same start time (`record.date`, to the millisecond) and answers `saved: false`. No schema change. A ride with no date (none are made any more) is never merged.

## Virtual gears: offset and shift strength (13/11/2026)
- The gear effect is now an offset worked out from the real road gradient: `gearOffset` = gear-matched gradient − road. The trainer gets road × difficulty + offset × shift strength, clamped to the mode's range. At 100% difficulty and 100% strength this equals the first gear model exactly (tested against its outputs).
- Why: before, the gear maths ran on the difficulty-scaled road, so at low difficulty a gear felt different on the same hill. Now difficulty only softens the hill and each gear always moves the trainer by the same amount.
- Solo Slope mode has no difficulty, so it uses 100%.
- Shift strength: 50 to 300% in 10% steps, default 100%, one per-device setting (Settings, free ride header, Slope mode header) read on every trainer update. It exists because the trainer's own sim model differs from ours, so a real ride may need gears to bite harder.
- Below 20 rpm the offset is 0, so the trainer gets road × difficulty.
- The gear is shown as a big tile with the gradient actually sent to the trainer; it flashes red and nudges up on a harder gear, green and down on an easier one. The small gear text in the gradient box and the Slope stepper is gone, and the gradient box no longer repeats the trainer gradient.
- `trainerGrade` in `core/physics.ts` and `gearText` had no callers left and were deleted; their difficulty test now runs through `gearGrade` at zero cadence.
- Hardware: Click v1 firmware 1.1.0 works; 1.0.0 ignores RideOn. On 1.1.0 a handshake write with response fails, so the driver keeps writing without response.

## Downloads through the server (2026-11-14)

## Strava (2026-11-14)
- Policy: Cranky only uploads to Strava. It asks for `activity:write` alone, reads nothing back beyond the athlete's name on connecting, and shows no Strava data to anyone but the rider who connected. Strava lets an app have 10 connected athletes before it needs Strava's review, which covers the crew; more riders than that needs the review first.
- Tokens live in a `strava` table on the server and never reach the client or the log. The connect click makes a single-use state tied to the rider that lasts 10 minutes (`STRAVA_STATE_S`); the callback has no login header, so the state is the only thing that says who came back. An access token with under an hour left is refreshed before use. Disconnect asks Strava to deauthorise (best effort: if Strava can't be reached, the tokens are deleted anyway).
- The Connect button was first drawn in CSS; it is now Strava's own button (see the brand assets entry below).
- Strava calls use httpx (now a runtime dependency, already used by the tests): multipart uploads and mocked transports in tests take far less code than urllib.
- Rate limits: every call reads Strava's usage headers and stops 10 short of either limit (`STRAVA_RATE_MARGIN`) until the next quarter hour (or midnight UTC for the daily one); a 429 does the same.
- Uploads: the device builds the .fit exactly as Download .fit does (Strava gets the same file) and sends it, base64 in JSON, to `POST /api/strava/upload/{ride}` once the server has the ride. `POST /api/rides` now also answers with the ride's number, and the ride queue hands each saved ride on, so a ride saved later (offline at the finish) uploads too. The server keeps the job and the file in a `strava_uploads` table until it is done, so a restart loses nothing; the file is dropped once Strava has it.
- A ride goes up once: a job that is done or on its way is left alone when the same ride is sent again, and Strava saying "duplicate of activity N" counts as done with that activity. The `external_id` is `cranky-<rider>-<ride number>`.
- Retries: a network error, Strava's 5xx or a refused token waits 1 minute, then 2, 4… up to an hour; after 8 tries the ride shows Retry. A rate limit waits for the next window and doesn't use a try. Strava has 5 minutes to process a file (checked every 4 s) before that counts as a failed try. Strava saying no for good (4xx) fails straight away with its message.
- Titles: the bus is "🧟 Zombie Bus · plan · survived with a 42 m gap" (or "caught" when the armour is gone: the bus never stops, so caught means it finished wrecked); the gap is the one at the finish. To have it, and each rider's share of the work, the bus result now also keeps the final `gap` and each rider's `kj` (from the server's ride stats). VO2 Madness is "🔥 VO2 Madness · plan", a free or group ride is the route's name, a solo workout "💪 plan · Cranky workout".
- Postcard links: Strava's API can't attach photos, so each ride sent with its postcard gets a public page `/p/<id>` (a 16-byte random id, kept across retries) showing the picture, with `og:image` (absolute, from `PUBLIC_URL`) so the link in the description previews as the picture. The page answers only once the ride is on Strava; the PNG is kept for good. The postcard is the one History rebuilds (a bus ride's crew card from its saved result, any other ride's card on the sky with the power skyline), built on the device and sent with the .fit. On the results screen, "Save postcard for Strava" (shown once connected) saves the postcard with its scene still through the usual download link, so iOS's share sheet can add it to the activity by hand.
- History: the list shows each ride's Strava state in a word ("Strava", "Uploading", "Strava: retry"), and the ride itself has the actions: View on Strava, Retry, or Upload to Strava for a ride from before connecting. The actions aren't in the list because each list row is one big button, and a button can't hold another. A ride on its way is checked again every 5 s while it is open. `GET /api/strava/rides` gives a rider only their own uploads.

## Crew game kit (2026-11-14)
- A crew game is a subclass of `CrewGame` (`modes/common.py`); Zombie Bus and VO2 Madness now inherit it for the defaults and are otherwise untouched.
- A game's band is fractions of FTP; the server turns it into watts for each rider and clips both edges at 150% FTP (`TARGET_CAP`, the same cap plan targets had inline). `tgt` is the middle of the band so every screen that already shows a target keeps working.
- Game inputs (`act`) are limited to 8 in 4 s per rider (`ACT_LIMIT`): enough for emoji spam to feel live, not enough to flood a room. Extra ones are dropped silently.
- A never-ERG game reports the ride's trainer mode as `gears`, which the client already obeys, so forcing ERG off needed no client change.
- `mode.on_act` takes the room as well as the rider (`on_act(room, rider, k, v)`): cards and votes aim at other riders.
- Scripted sessions (`modes/script.py`): a game's segments become a plan of `free` blocks, so the lobby, clock and results treat it like any workout and no block can carry an ERG target. Events start 40 to 75 s after the last one ended (`SCRIPT_GAP_S`), never the same kind twice running, and end 5 s before their segment does. "Something happening" for the 90 s rule means an event running or starting, or a segment starting; each game's tests hold the worst case over a few hundred seeds to 90 s (`script.worst_gap`). So warm-ups and cool-downs need events too, even if they are only banter.
- Two ways to make a game short for tests: `ZB_TIME_SCALE` (already there) runs the clock fast, and the new `ZB_SCRIPT_SCALE` shrinks the script itself (0.125 turns 40 minutes into 5, events and gaps included). - Power shape (`ride_stats.py`), worked out once a second from the power the server receives: the gap between a rider's 3 s power and their own mean 3 s power over the last 30 s. The mean gap as a share of FTP is the rider's spread (low is smooth); a spike is each time 3 s power jumps more than 15% of FTP above that mean (`SPIKE_PCT`), counted once however long it lasts, and dropping power is never a spike. `rs_dev` is the gap right now, for meters like Comfort. A change of target reads as a gap too, so a game that only wants to blame riders for spikes they chose has to look at `rs_dev` in its steady stretches itself.
- Punished time is a plain counter of seconds that games add to (`Rider.punish`). The shared awards (`shape_awards`) are smoothest (lowest spread, needs 60 s of riding), spikiest (most spikes) and most punished; a game passes its own three names.
